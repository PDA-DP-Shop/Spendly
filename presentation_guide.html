💬 Line-by-Line Presentation Dialogue Script

👑 1. INTRODUCTION & WELCOME (Devansh starts)

[Devansh stands up, clicks the first slide, and speaks with confidence]

"Good morning Mam and friends. I am Devansh Patel, the team leader for our Semester 7 Computer Engineering project: Spendly. Today, my friends Udit, Meet, Rudra, and I are going to show you a system that changes how local business billing and expense management works.

Standard financial apps read our private bank message alerts and send our transaction details to remote cloud databases. This centralized model compromises user privacy and is prone to security leaks. Additionally, small retailers have to pay monthly database subscription fees to sustain POS registers.

To solve these challenges, we built Spendly, a zero-knowledge, offline-first peer-to-peer ecosystem. It consists of three progressive web apps: Spendly User, Spendly Shop, and Spendly Radar. All transaction history is stored locally in browser enclaves on the device. Let's look at Spendly User first."
📱 2. SPENDLY USER DEMO (Devansh)

[Devansh transitions to the app screenshot or live screen]

"Spendly User is the shopper's personal expense wallet. Because it is offline-first, all data is committed to a browser sandbox database using IndexedDB with the Dexie.js wrapper. To keep data secure on the device, we integrated the hardware-backed WebAuthn API (Passkeys). Users can register biometrics (Touch ID / Face ID) to decrypt local databases without cloud servers.

For cash management, we built a Cash Wallet Note Tracker. Instead of entering simple numbers, users input the count of their ₹500, ₹200, ₹100, and ₹50 notes, and the app calculates balances dynamically.

Another feature is the Clipboard SMS alert parser. Instead of requesting intrusive background SMS permissions, users copy transaction alerts to the clipboard, and our regex engine parses details (merchant, amount, debit/credit flag) locally in memory.

Lastly, we have a Shagun Gift Registry. It tracks reciprocal cash gifts exchanged during weddings and festivals. Now, let's look at the cashier POS billing system. Udit, please explain."
🛒

Udit

Merchant POS Register

[Udit takes over the slides and addresses the review panel]

"Thank you, Devansh. Hello Mam, I am Udit, and I will demonstrate how the merchant Point-of-Sale checkout register works.

Spendly Shop is designed as a browser cash register used by cashiers. When the register scans a product barcode via the camera scanner, it sends a PRODUCT_REQUEST query packet to our paired Spendly Radar hub. Radar queries its catalog database and returns product descriptions and prices. Shop then calculates tax lines (CGST/SGST/IGST), applies active campaigns, and compiles the final invoice.

For bill transmission, we built a Billing Beam pipeline: 1. Web NFC Beam: Cashier taps the register against the shopper's phone to transfer the invoice JSON payload directly. 2. Paired WebRTC Stream: For paired devices, invoice objects are encrypted with AES-256-GCM and sent over WebRTC. 3. Dynamic QR Code: If NFC is unavailable, the register displays a dynamic QR code on screen containing the raw invoice data, which the customer scans.

Let's see how Radar manages store catalogs. Meet, over to you."
📡

Meet

Radar Hub & Promotions

[Meet steps up to demonstrate the Radar Hub screens]

"Thanks, Udit. Good morning Mam, I am Meet, and I will walk you through Spendly Radar.

Radar is run by the store manager on a central tablet or PC. It manages inventory directories (db.radarStock) and store offers (db.radarOffers).

A key feature is the Offers Validation Claims loop. When a user claims an offer on their phone, they receive a 6-digit claim PIN. The cashier enters this PIN on the POS register, which sends an OFFER_VALIDATE_REQUEST to Radar. Radar verifies the code, invalidates it, and returns confirmation.

Additionally, we built a Privacy-Preserving Analytics module. Shoppers' devices reverse-geocode location locally, strip all personal details, and send transaction amounts anonymously to local Radar nodes to display area trend charts.

Now, Rudra will explain how our P2P connection channels function."
⛓️

Rudra

P2P Network Infrastructure

[Rudra presents the system architecture and network connection slides]

"Thank you, Meet. Hello Mam, I am Rudra, and I will explain the networking and infrastructure layers of Spendly.

To keep the system zero-cost, we implemented three synchronization methods: 1. LocalStorage Same-Device Bridge: If the merchant open apps in different tabs on the same browser, they sync data with $0 latency by listening to browser Window StorageEvents on key 'spendly_bridge_msg'. 2. WebRTC PeerJS channels: When running on different devices, the apps establish peer-to-peer data channels. Handshake signaling is handled by public WebSocket topics on ntfy.sh. 3. Serverless Matching & Backups: A Cloudflare Worker hosts matchmaking keys in Worker KV namespaces to pair devices, clearing the keys after 10 minutes.

By combining IndexedDB, WebRTC, and biometric WebAuthn keys, we built a responsive, zero-cost consumer ecosystem. That concludes our presentation. Thank you, Mam, and we are ready for your questions."

💡 Quick Presentation Tips for Team Success

  • Practice the transitions: Practice passing the mic smoothly to Udit, Meet, and Rudra.
  • Keep the local setup ready: Open all three tabs in one browser window before the viva begins, so you can demonstrate the localStorage bridge sync.
  • Emphasize Security: Mention WebAuthn and IndexedDB to demonstrate on-device data safety.
  • Emphasize Cost Savings: Explain how WebRTC and Cloudflare Workers remove database hosting costs.
